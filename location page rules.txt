Group G needs careful handling

Two different things are in this group and they follow different rules.

"Onward Psychiatry" is the business name. Use it freely. It appears in the title tag, the footer, the address block, and throughout the copy. Always capitalized, always spelled exactly this way.

"Onward psychiatry solutions" is a search query, not a business name. People type it, so the page should answer it, but the practice is not called Onward Psychiatry Solutions. Never write it as a capitalized three word proper noun, because that reads as a second business name and creates a mismatch with the Google Business Profile, the directory citations, and the schema markup. Local rankings depend on that name matching everywhere it appears.

Target the query instead by putting the words near each other in a sentence where "solutions" is doing an ordinary job:

"Onward Psychiatry offers TMS therapy solutions in Norwood, MA for people whose medication has not worked."

"The mental health solutions Onward Psychiatry provides in Norwood go beyond medication management."

"Looking at treatment solutions? Onward Psychiatry in Norwood, MA offers FDA cleared TMS therapy."

Google matches queries on meaning and word proximity, not on an exact character string, so a phrasing like these picks up the query without inventing a brand.

Never place it in: the title tag, the H1, the footer, the address block, image alt text, or anywhere the business identifies itself by name. Two or three uses in body copy is the ceiling.

4\. Audience and intent

Someone in or near Norwood who has been on antidepressants, usually two or more, and is still depressed. They are tired, skeptical, and have probably been told to try a different SSRI more than once. They are working out whether TMS is real, whether it is covered, and whether it is worth 35 trips to a clinic on Walpole Street.

Write for that person. Not for a search engine, and not for a clinician.

They must walk away knowing: what TMS is, that it is FDA cleared and not ECT, that a session is about 19 minutes with no downtime, that insurance usually covers it and the clinic handles the paperwork, where the office is, and what the first step is.

5\. Voice and style

Plain, warm, direct. Second person, present tense.

Short sentences. Paragraphs of 2 to 4 sentences.

No hype. Avoid revolutionary, life changing, cutting edge, game changer.

No fear based framing. Do not describe depression vividly to manufacture urgency.

Reading level grade 8 to 9\. US English.

Write like a person, not like a chatbot

Copy that sounds machine written loses trust fast, and on a medical page that matters more than usual. Avoid the following.

Words and phrases to cut: delve, navigate the landscape, embark, unlock, realm, robust, seamless, leverage, foster, elevate, empower, transformative, holistic, tapestry, testament, beacon, cornerstone, pave the way, dive into, in today's fast paced world, when it comes to, rest assured, look no further, it is important to note, that said.

Sentence shapes to avoid:

"It is not just X, it is Y." Say what it is.

A colon followed by a dramatic reveal.

Rhetorical questions used as headings or openers.

Three item lists in every paragraph. Vary the rhythm.

Starting consecutive sentences with "Whether you are."

Moreover, Furthermore, Additionally as sentence openers.

No em dashes anywhere in the copy. Use a comma, a full stop, a colon, or rewrite the sentence. If you find yourself reaching for one, the sentence is usually doing two jobs and should be two sentences. Middle dots in the badge and chip rows are fine, since those are list separators rather than prose.

Note that some locked content already contains words on this list. Locked content stays exactly as it is. The rule applies to copy you write.

Medical and YMYL rules, non negotiable

Never promise or imply a specific outcome. Write "many patients experience," not "you will feel."

Never claim TMS is better than medication or therapy. It is an option when medication has not worked.

Never state or imply that insurance will definitely cover a given reader.

Do not invent statistics, response rates or study references. Use only the figures in Part 6\.

Do not give clinical advice about stopping or changing medication.

Specific to the outcomes keywords in Group D: "success rate" is the riskiest term on this page. Anchor every use to the supplied NeuroStar figure and attribute it. Write "in NeuroStar's clinical data, 83% of patients experienced meaningful symptom relief." Never write "our success rate is 83%," which claims practice level outcomes the client cannot substantiate.
