# Genetic Testing for Psychiatric Medications

Finding a psychiatric medication that works often takes weeks. Many people try multiple prescriptions, adjusting doses while coping with side effects or waiting for relief that never comes. Genetic testing, also called pharmacogenomic (PGx) testing, gives your psychiatric clinician biological data to make informed prescribing choices from the start.

At Onward Psychiatry in Norwood, MA, we use a simple cheek swab to see how your body processes psychiatric medications. By looking at specific liver enzymes and brain receptors, we can select medications that fit your biology.

&nbsp;

## How Pharmacogenomic Testing Works

Every person breaks down medicine at a different speed. That speed determines how much medication reaches your bloodstream and how long it stays there. Pharmacogenomic testing looks at two biological pathways:

* **Pharmacokinetics (drug metabolism):** Most psychiatric medications pass through the Cytochrome P450 (CYP450) enzyme system in your liver. The test shows whether you break down specific drugs poorly, normally, or rapidly. If your liver breaks down a drug too slowly, standard doses can build up and trigger severe side effects. If your liver clears it too quickly, standard doses leave your body before relieving your symptoms.
* **Pharmacodynamics (brain receptor response):** The test checks variations in genes tied to serotonin transport, dopamine breakdown, and folate processing. These markers show whether your brain receptors will respond to specific medications as expected.

During your visit, a staff member swabs the inside of your cheek. We send the sample to an accredited medical genetics laboratory for analysis.

## Conditions Supported by Genetic Testing

Genetic testing helps guide medication plans across several conditions, especially when standard treatments cause harsh side effects or fail to work.

* **Major Depressive Disorder (MDD):** Antidepressants like SSRIs and SNRIs rely on specific liver enzymes. Testing shows which antidepressants your body can process normally, helping your clinician choose an effective option faster.
* **Treatment-Resistant Depression:** If you have tried two or more antidepressants without success, testing can pinpoint metabolic issues or receptor variations that explain why earlier medications failed.
* **Anxiety Disorders:** For generalized anxiety, panic disorder, or social anxiety, test results help clinicians pick medications that avoid early spikes in jitteriness or daytime drowsiness.
* **Bipolar Disorder:** Balancing mood stabilizers and antipsychotics requires precision. Genetic data flags medications with higher risks of adverse reactions.
* **ADHD:** Testing shows how you process stimulant and non-stimulant medications, which helps your provider choose appropriate starting doses.

## What Genetic Testing Can and Cannot Tell Us

Genetic testing provides practical information, but it has clear boundaries.

### What Testing Can Do

* Show whether your liver breaks down specific medications unusually fast or slowly.
* Flag medications likely to cause side effects at typical starting doses.
* Add objective biological data to your clinical evaluation and medical history.
* Help your clinician pick safer starting doses based on your metabolism.

### What Testing Cannot Do

* It cannot diagnose mental health conditions. A cheek swab cannot tell if you have depression, anxiety, or bipolar disorder.
* It cannot guarantee a medication will work. A drug that fits your metabolism can still fail because of stress, sleep, physical health, or life events.
* It does not replace clinical evaluations. Test results are one tool among several that guide your provider's choices.

## The Testing Process at Onward Psychiatry

### Step 1: Clinical Consultation

During your visit at our Norwood clinic or over telehealth, your provider reviews your psychiatric history, current symptoms, and past medication trials. If testing makes clinical sense, your provider explains how the results will guide your care.

### Step 2: Cheek Swab Collection

Collecting your DNA takes about thirty seconds. A clinical assistant rubs a soft cotton swab against the inside of your cheek. You do not need needles, blood draws, or fasting.

### Step 3: Lab Analysis

We send your sample to a CLIA-certified and CAP-accredited clinical genetics lab. The lab analyzes your sample against peer-reviewed guidelines from the Clinical Pharmacogenetics Implementation Consortium (CPIC) and the FDA.

### Step 4: Results Review and Treatment Plan

Results arrive in 7 to 10 business days. Your clinician walks you through the report in plain language. Together, you decide on medication changes or starting doses based on the lab findings.

## Who Is a Good Candidate for Genetic Testing?

You may benefit from genetic testing if you:

* Have tried multiple psychiatric medications without finding adequate relief.
* Experience strong or intolerable side effects from standard doses.
* Need unusually high doses of medication before noticing any benefit.
* Are starting psychiatric medication for the first time and want to avoid unnecessary trial periods.
* Take multiple daily prescriptions and need to check for drug-gene interactions.

&nbsp;

## Benefits of Testing

* **Fewer trial periods:** Providers pick medications suited to your metabolism, cutting out unneeded trial runs with drugs your body cannot process well.
* **Lower risk of side effects:** Knowing you metabolize a drug slowly allows clinicians to lower your dose or pick an alternative, preventing medications from building up to toxic levels.
* **Clearer dosing:** If you clear a drug quickly, your provider knows to prescribe a therapeutic dose right away instead of waiting weeks on a sub-therapeutic dose.
* **Clearer direction:** Having concrete biological data helps eliminate guesswork and explains why past medications caused problems.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How is the genetic test done?

A clinician rubs a cotton swab along the inside of your cheek for about thirty seconds. The collection is painless, takes less than a minute, and requires no blood draw or advance preparation.

### Does genetic testing guarantee that a medication will cure my symptoms?

No. The test shows how your body breaks down medications and how your receptors may respond. This helps rule out drugs likely to cause bad side effects or fail because of rapid metabolism. Personal history, therapy, and daily habits still play a major role in your recovery.

### Will my health insurance cover genetic testing?

Many insurance plans cover genetic testing if you have a diagnosed condition like Major Depressive Disorder and have tried at least one or two medications that did not help. The testing lab verifies your coverage before processing the sample.

### How long do results take?

Results usually arrive within 7 to 10 business days after the lab receives your swab. Once the results are in, we schedule a follow-up visit to review the findings.

### Can this test diagnose depression or anxiety?

No. Pharmacogenomic testing only analyzes how you metabolize medications. It cannot diagnose depression, anxiety, ADHD, or bipolar disorder. Diagnoses require a psychiatric evaluation with a licensed provider.

### Should I stop taking my current medication before the test?

No. Keep taking your prescribed medications. Your DNA does not change, so medications will not alter your test results. Never stop or change psychiatric medications without talking to your prescriber.

### How does this differ from commercial DNA ancestry kits?

Ancestry kits focus on family roots and general wellness traits. Psychiatric genetic testing is an accredited medical diagnostic panel that analyzes specific genes involved in drug metabolism and brain receptors.

### How does Onward Psychiatry use my results?

Your clinician reviews the report with you, explaining which medications fit your metabolic profile and which ones carry higher risks of side effects. We use that information to adjust your current medications or select a new option.
