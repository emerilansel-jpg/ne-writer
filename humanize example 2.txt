# **Deployment Mental Health Support**

**FDA-Cleared for Post-Deployment Depression and Anxious Depression · Non-Drug · NeuroStar®**

Returning home from a deployment rarely feels like a clean break. Service members turn in their gear, sign out on block leave, and step back into everyday life with family and friends. Yet for many, the body and mind stay on edge. Sleep remains fragmented, loud public places feel overwhelming, and an emotional numbness sets in that makes connecting with loved ones difficult.

These challenges are not a failure of will or a lack of resilience. Months spent in high-stress operational environments alter how the brain functions, often developing into persistent depression and anxiety long after the rotation ends. Fayetteville TMS provides an evidence-based, drug-free therapy designed to help restore balance to underactive brain pathways.

## **What NeuroStar TMS Treats**

Post-deployment mental health rarely involves just one clear issue. Service members often experience a combination of low mood, high anxiety, sleep disruption, and the emotional toll of traumatic events.

Fayetteville TMS believes in complete clinical transparency:

* **FDA-Cleared Uses:** NeuroStar Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (TMS) is cleared by the FDA to treat Major Depressive Disorder, anxious depression, and OCD in adults.  
* **Off-Label Boundaries:** TMS is not cleared by the FDA as a primary treatment for post-traumatic stress disorder, traumatic brain injuries, or substance use disorders.

For individuals living with both trauma and depression, treating the depressive symptoms can still make a substantial difference. Over half of service members dealing with combat-related trauma also meet the clinical criteria for depression. When clinical depression leaves a person feeling exhausted and emotionally drained, trauma-focused therapies can feel impossible to start. By lifting the heavy weight of depression first, patients often regain the mental energy needed to engage in counseling.

## **Understanding the Post-Deployment Nervous System**

The physical and emotional shifts that follow a deployment stem from direct changes in brain biology:

* **Persistent High Alert:** Downrange, staying constantly aware of surroundings is essential for survival. Back home, that internal alarm system often fails to shut off. Over time, that constant tension drains essential brain chemicals tied to energy and mood.  
* **Disrupted Sleep Patterns:** Broken sleep during deployments deprives the body of deep physical recovery. When irregular sleep continues back home, it directly feeds into daytime brain fog, short tempers, and depressive episodes.  
* **The Impact of Multiple Rotations:** Consecutive tours add up. A service member may feel relatively steady after an initial deployment, only to find that subsequent rotations compound emotional strain and make readjustment harder.

## **A Non-Drug Option for Hard-to-Treat Depression**

Many service members seek help only to find that standard antidepressant medications do not resolve their symptoms. Some medications produce flat emotions or physical fatigue, while others simply lose effectiveness over time. When two or more medications fail to provide relief, the condition is considered treatment-resistant depression.

As an essential component of comprehensive [mental health support for military service members](https://fayettevilletms.com/who-we-serve/military/), NeuroStar TMS provides a non-systemic approach that works directly on brain circuitry without circulating through the bloodstream:

* **Focused Magnetic Pulses:** The therapy delivers gentle magnetic fields to the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, the exact region that runs slow during depressive episodes.  
* **Free from Systemic Side Effects:** Because the treatment is non-chemical, patients do not experience weight changes, grogginess, brain fog, or sexual side effects.  
* **Maintains Daily Routines:** Each outpatient session lasts roughly 19 minutes, five days a week, over six weeks. Patients remain alert throughout the visit and drive themselves immediately back to work or family responsibilities.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

### **Can service members receive deployment mental health support through TMS while participating in VA therapy?**

Yes. Many service members and veterans choose to receive TMS therapy for depression while simultaneously engaged in trauma counseling or cognitive therapies through the VA or civilian providers. Because TMS requires no reprocessing of past traumatic memories, easing the underlying depressive fog often makes evidence-based PTSD treatments far more productive. With written patient authorization, the clinical team coordinates care directly with existing VA healthcare providers.

### **Does TMS therapy cause downtime or side effects that impact active-duty readiness?**

No. NeuroStar TMS is a non-invasive, non-drug procedure with zero systemic side effects, sedation, or cognitive impairment. Mild scalp tenderness or a minor headache may occur during the first week of sessions, but individuals remain fully alert throughout every visit and can drive immediately back to work, physical training, or personal activities.

### **How does TRICARE cover deployment mental health support through TMS?**

TRICARE covers TMS therapy for Major Depressive Disorder when documented clinical criteria are met, typically including a trial of standard antidepressant medications that failed to provide relief. Operational history or deployment status serves as clinical background, while insurance authorization is determined by medical necessity. The intake staff handles all prior authorization documentation directly with TRICARE.

### **Can veterans use VA Community Care for post-deployment TMS therapy?**

Yes. Enrolled veterans receiving care through the Fayetteville VA Medical Center may qualify for TMS under the VA Community Care Network when local VA clinics cannot provide timely access or specialized outpatient options. The administrative team coordinates directly with the VA Community Care office to manage approvals, schedule visits, and ensure zero out-of-pocket surprise costs.

### **Will receiving post-deployment mental health care affect security clearance status?**

Routine outpatient mental health care for depression or anxiety generally does not affect security clearance eligibility. Current federal guidelines under SF-86 Question 21 focus on conditions involving inpatient hospitalizations or involuntary commitments rather than voluntary outpatient support. Seeking proactive care helps resolve debilitating symptoms early, preserving both personal health and long-term military career progression.
