# **TMS Therapy for Active-Duty Service Members**

Serving on active duty involves high-stakes responsibilities, strict readiness standards, and complex administrative requirements. Managing clinical care around early morning formations, physical training cycles, shift work, field exercises, or deployment preparation can present logistical challenges for service members and their military medical managers.

When major depressive disorder or co-occurring symptoms persist despite trials of standard health interventions, finding practical clinical support is essential. Fayetteville TMS provides advanced TMS therapy for active-duty service members, delivering an FDA-cleared, non-drug outpatient option for clinical depression. At Fayetteville TMS, our civilian team offers flexible appointments, off-post access, and complete coordination support for TRICARE insurance benefits.

## **Understanding Active-Duty Mental Health Realities and Operational Strain**

Operational demands within military life rarely align neatly with medical treatment windows. Active-duty personnel often manage cumulative physical and psychological stressors, including shifting duty hours, prolonged operational tempo, and personal expectations surrounding performance.

Depression within military populations can impair concentration, decision-making, and functional readiness. Addressing mental health concerns through qualified medical channels supports long-term personal resilience and overall well-being.

For some service members, oral antidepressant medications may present side effects such as fatigue, weight shifts, or gastrointestinal distress. When prior medication trials do not yield satisfactory symptom relief, clinical teams may evaluate treatment-resistant depression protocols to determine if an advanced outpatient intervention is appropriate.

## **NeuroStar® TMS as an Advanced Outpatient Option**

NeuroStar® TMS is an FDA-cleared, non-drug neurostimulation treatment designed to address the biological mechanisms associated with major depression. During chronic depressive states, neural activity in specific mood-regulating regions of the brain, such as the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, may become underactive.

NeuroStar® TMS uses magnetic pulses to stimulate these targeted brain regions, encouraging neural pathways to resume normal activity over time.

Because magnetic stimulation operates locally rather than systemically through the bloodstream, patients avoid side effects commonly associated with oral medications, such as sedation or systemic toxicity. Patients remain awake and alert throughout each session and drive themselves to and from appointments, making TMS therapy for active-duty service members a workable clinical option around routine unit responsibilities.

### **FDA-Cleared Indications and Clinical Scope**

It is clinically important to distinguish conditions for which NeuroStar® TMS is FDA-cleared from co-occurring conditions that require separate management:

* **Major Depressive Disorder (MDD):** FDA-cleared for adult patients who have not achieved satisfactory improvement from prior antidepressant medication trials.  
* **MDD with Anxious Depression:** FDA-cleared for adult patients presenting with major depression accompanied by anxiety symptoms.  
* **Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD):** FDA-cleared as an adjunctive non-drug treatment protocol for adults.  
* **Adolescent Depression:** FDA-cleared for eligible adolescents aged 15 to 21 as an adjunctive treatment.

**Clinical & Regulatory Note:** NeuroStar® TMS is not indicated or FDA-cleared as a standalone treatment for Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD), Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI), or substance use disorders. However, when these conditions coexist with major depression, treating underlying depressive symptoms may support overall stability as part of a multi-disciplinary treatment plan.

## **Privacy, Administrative Guidelines, and Cross-Branch Care Options**

Understanding how civilian care interacts with military administrative policies helps set realistic expectations before initiating treatment.

### **Civilian Medical Documentation and MHS Genesis**

Care provided at Fayetteville TMS is recorded within secure civilian electronic health record systems governed by federal HIPAA laws. Civilian medical records do not automatically transfer into military health systems like MHS Genesis. However, records may be shared or accessed when official TRICARE claims are processed, when PCM referrals are submitted, or when an explicit medical release is authorized.

### **Command Notification Standards (DoDI 6490.08)**

Under Department of Defense Instruction (DoDI) 6490.08, healthcare providers evaluate command notification requirements based on specific clinical criteria, such as acute safety risks, severe occupational impairment, or inpatient admission needs. Routine outpatient care generally aims to respect patient privacy within standard medical guidelines, though final administrative or reporting requirements depend on evaluations by military medical authorities. Service members should discuss any branch-specific reporting rules with their care team.

### **Security Clearances (SF-86 Question 21\) and Special Duty Status**

* **Security Clearances (SF-86 Question 21):** Standard Form 86 contains specific reporting parameters regarding mental health care. Because official question carve-outs apply only to limited situations (such as marital, family, or grief counseling not tied to violence, as well as specific trauma-related care), undergoing clinical treatment for conditions like major depressive disorder generally requires affirmative disclosure. However, federal adjudicative guidelines emphasize that voluntarily seeking professional care demonstrates personal reliability and responsible health maintenance, and treatment alone rarely leads to clearance denial. Personnel are encouraged to review current Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency (DCSA) instructions or consult their unit security manager regarding individual disclosure questions.  
* **Special Duty & Aviation Status:** Service members on flight status, special operations billets, or special access programs must coordinate care plans with their Flight Surgeon or assigned Medical Officer to ensure compliance with specific aeromedical or program regulations.

## **Step-by-Step Evaluation and TRICARE Navigation**

We work to make the intake and benefit verification process clear and structured:

1. **Initial Consultation:** Contact our office to review clinical history, daily schedule requirements, and treatment goals.  
2. **Clinical Evaluation:** A qualified mental health professional conducts a comprehensive assessment to evaluate MDD severity and screen for safety factors, such as conductive or magnetic implants.  
3. **TRICARE Benefit Verification:** Our administrative team assists with pre-authorization and referral verification:  
   * **TRICARE Prime:** Typically requires a referral from your Primary Care Manager (PCM) or Military Treatment Facility (MTF) provider, followed by pre-authorization through Humana Military.  
   * **TRICARE Select & Reserve Select:** Pre-authorization is required prior to initiating care, though referral rules differ from Prime protocols.  
   * **VA Community Care Network (CCN):** Veterans utilizing CCN benefits require an official referral generated by their VA care team.  
   * *Coverage Note:* Coverage, copayments, cost-shares, and deductibles vary based on plan tier, beneficiary status, network participation, and annual catastrophic cap status.  
4. **Motor Threshold Mapping:** The initial treatment session determines individualized scalp placement and magnetic dose levels.  
5. **Daily Outpatient Treatment:** Sessions take place 5 days a week for approximately 6 weeks. Patients remain seated, awake, and alert throughout.  
6. **Progress Monitoring:** Clinical assessment scales (such as the PHQ-9) are completed periodically to evaluate symptom progress.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

**Do I Need Prior Command Approval to Begin Treatment?**

Generally, service members access civilian outpatient healthcare through standard medical referral channels under their TRICARE plan. However, specialized duty roles, flight clearance requirements, or specific unit guidelines may require coordination with your PCM or military medical officer.

**Will Receiving TMS Impact My Security Clearance?**

Seeking medical care for clinical depression is recognized by security clearance adjudicators as a positive sign of health maintenance and personal responsibility. While clinical treatment for major depression is typically reportable on standard security questionnaires (such as SF-86 Question 21), treatment itself is evaluated in the context of overall reliability, judgment, and adherence to medical guidance.

**Does Treatment Require a Temporary Duty Profile?**

Receiving TMS therapy for active-duty service members does not automatically generate a duty limitation profile or Medical Evaluation Board (MEB) referral. Because treatment is non-sedating, many service members maintain their standard duty schedules. Formal duty profile decisions remain under the authority of military medical personnel.

**How Do Appointments Fit Around Duty Schedules?**

Treatment sessions last between 19 and 40 minutes. Our clinic offers early morning and late afternoon appointment times to help accommodate duty hours, shift work, and unit schedules.
